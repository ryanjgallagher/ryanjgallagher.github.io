<Experience spaceAfter="2.5rem" />


I am an applied scientist in online trust & safety. I specialize in using machine learning and 
data science to detect scaled abuse and platform manipulation. In particular, my work 
often focuses on deception and networked harms, such as impersonation, inauthenticity,
spam, and covert influence operations. Lately, I have been interested in
better detecting online frauds and scams, which are executed using many of the same deceptive 
techniques deployed by other harmful actors. I am comfortable working across the entire data science 
and machine learning stack. From statistical analyses and production-ready models to threat 
assessments and strategy building, my background has prepared me to wear many hats to do what is
needed and keep people safe online.

I hold a [Ph.D. in network science](https://www.networkscienceinstitute.org/) from Northeastern
University, where I conducted research with 
[Professor Brooke Foucault Welles](http://brooke-welles.squarespace.com/) as part of
the [Communication Media and Marginalization (CoMM) Lab](https://www.networkscienceinstitute.org/commlab).
My research brought together network science and communication theory to study how individuals use 
social media to amplify their voices. A core focus of my work was 
[online activism](/publications/?topic=online-activism) and how political movements construct
narratives both over time and at critical moments. To conduct this research, I often made methodological
advances in [network science](/publications/?topic=network-science) and 
[natural language processing](/publications/?topic=natural-language-processing). Public sphere
theory, networked (counter)publics, and communication studies are all still very near and dear to 
my heart.

During my Ph.D., I interned with Facebook's Core Data Science team, where I worked on measuring the 
reach of coordinated information operations and their audiences. Before Northeastern, I obtained an 
M.S. in mathematics at the University of Vermont. At UVM, I worked with the 
[Computational Story Lab](https://www.uvm.edu/storylab/) and the 
[Vermont Complex Systems Center](https://vermontcomplexsystems.org/) under the 
guidance of [Professor Chris Danforth](https://cdanfort.w3.uvm.edu/) and 
[Professor Peter Dodds](https://www.uvm.edu/cems/cs/profile/peter-dodds). I also hold a B.A. in 
mathematics from the University of Connecticut.

These days, I spend some time working on [open source projects](https://github.com/ryanjgallagher),
some of which try to atone for the coding sins I committed during graduate school. While finishing
my Ph.D., I picked up an affinity for soulslike and rougelike games because they were easier than
finishing my dissertation. The rest of my free time is spent playing fingerstyle guitar and taking
care of my two cats, Ember and Mabel (names inspired by _Dark Souls_ and _Only Murders in the 
Building_, respectively).
